Motion designer. Systems thinker. The long road to Green Light Studios.

I'm Alex, the motion designer behind Green Light Studios. Most designers think in stories. I also think in logical systems. That combination lives in every frame I build.

Based in Northern Colorado, working with businesses and studios across the USA and around the world. I specialize in motion graphics for brands that need to look like they belong.

The work I want to make is clean but surprisingly magical. Technically precise but emotionally felt. The kind of motion that makes a viewer pause mid scroll and rewind.

Background

The Full
Story

Some people find their path early. I had to lose mine first.

I graduated with a BFA in 3D animation with big dreams and bigger hopes. The student film I worked on was a semifinalist at the Student Academy Awards. But dreams and credentials mean nothing without a network, and I had neither. I spent two years trying to break into an industry that did not know I existed. Then I stopped trying and started selling paint. I told myself it must not have been meant to be.

It turned out I was wrong.

A family connection opened a door into technical consulting. I spent the next three years implementing enterprise software, learning how businesses operate from the inside, and building a brain for logical systems that art school could have never taught me. It paid the bills. It also quietly became one of the most useful things that ever happened to my creative work.

But something kept pulling at me.

I gave into it and took a risk on School of Motion. Intro to After Effects came first. Then Animation Bootcamp. Halfway through I almost quit again. Comparison is a silent killer and I let its roots burrow deep. I walked away a second time, letting the weight of past failures sweep me back into the dark.

I came back anyway. With trepidation and defiance I finished a piece called Eyes Go Here.

It was the best work I had ever made. Something locked into place that day that has not come loose since. The systems brain I built in consulting, the programming knowledge I had been quietly developing, the years of watching motion design from the outside. It all finally synced with the craft I had been training since college. It did not feel like starting over. It felt like waking up for the first time in years.

Most motion designers think in stories. I also think in logical systems. That is not a limiter of imagination. It is one of the greatest additions to it.

I have a programming certificate alongside my animation credentials. While most motion designers treat expressions like a foreign language, I write them like a native. That means I can build in ways others cannot, automate what others do by hand, and solve problems others do not know are solvable.
